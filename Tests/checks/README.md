# Pure checks

The parts of Sill that decide things (when the device looks for a Mac and which path a session
takes, the settings ledger, the wire format, what of the clipboard goes between the Mac and a device, pairing, who may use which door, which device versions a
Mac serves, what the Mac does with a trackpad gesture, how a session ends, how the device reads the
Mac's messages, what the update check makes of GitHub's answer, how frames go into the video encoder
and when a stream gets a new encoder session, who moves the Mac's pointer and what the device's
pointer sprite shows, how the Mac download's disk image lays out its window, where everything goes
on a phone held upright, which way a scroll on the device goes (Reverse Scrolling), which modifiers
a device's keys leave set on the Mac, what the Mac takes of a device's viewport, the pixel scale a capture runs at and where a device's position
lands on the Mac, when the device takes the keyboard for a hardware keyboard,
when it asks the Mac for a keyframe after its picture was interrupted, what an input method or
dictation sends the Mac and whose ⌃Space it is, what the Mac sends of its menus and what the device
makes of it, how the Mac's sound becomes packets and back and when and where the device plays them,
when the first-run tour shows and where its card goes, how tall the Mac's Settings window may be on
a screen, whether screen sharing shows the pairing window, which launches of the Mac app are Sill.app
itself and so take no test hook, pairing at home: who each door admits,
a screen, whether screen sharing shows the pairing window, pairing at home: who each door admits
and what waiting for admission costs,
what counts as the USB cable, the device's home rows and dials, where the layouts meet the iPhone
Duo's fold, when a window Sill sized goes back, which type encodings the virtual display's private
API may report, when the Mac's display is woken for a device, and whether the macOS firewall keeps
devices out) are plain Swift files that compile
on their own. Each folder here compiles one or a few of those files, exactly as they are in
when the first-run tour shows and where its card goes, how long a thumbnail is held for its lights, how tall the Mac's Settings window may be on
a screen, whether screen sharing shows the pairing window, pairing at home: who each door admits,
what counts as the USB cable, the device's home rows and dials, where the layouts meet the iPhone
Duo's fold, when a window Sill sized goes back, which type encodings the virtual display's private
API may report, when the Mac's display is woken for a device, and whether the catalog's look at the
displays will do for a capture or must be taken again) are plain Swift files that compile on their
own. Each folder here compiles one or a few of those files, exactly as they are in
`Sources/`, `iOSClient/` and `Scripts/`, together with its own `main.swift`, and runs the result.
Nothing here needs a device, Screen Recording, Accessibility, the video encoder or any network but
loopback, so the checks run anywhere Xcode does, and in CI
(`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) on pull requests and pushes to `main`.

```
Tests/checks/run-all.sh                   # every check, about five minutes on an M-series Mac
Tests/checks/run-all.sh policy fence      # only these
Tests/checks/policy/run.sh                # one check, with its whole output
Tests/checks/run-all.sh --mutants         # also the mutants: well over an hour
```

`run-all.sh` prints one line per check and, for a failing one, its `FAIL` lines and last lines; its
exit status is the number of checks that failed. Binaries, data and logs go to
`.build/checks/<name>/`. A check prints `ok` or `FAIL` per case and exits non-zero on any failure.

## Adding a check

A folder with a `main.swift` (prints `ok` or `FAIL` per case, exits non-zero on any failure) and a
`run.sh` that sets `here`, sources `../common.sh` (which sets `root` and `out` and changes to the
root) and compiles with `swiftc` into `"$out"`; or, for files that compile as one module with
StreamProtocol's sources, a `module.txt` naming them and a `run.sh` that hands the check to
`../module.py`, as `door-policy/run.sh` does. A `mutants.py` is optional; its last line must count
the mutants (`N of M mutants caught`, or `mutants caught: N of M`), and `run.sh --mutants` hands it
to `run_mutants`; add the check's name to the `mutants` matrix in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` too.
`run-all.sh` picks up every folder with a `run.sh`, and fails a check whose `run.sh` is not
executable (`chmod +x`; git keeps the bit) rather than skip it. Only pure files: nothing that needs
a permission, a device or the network, and never anything that links VideoToolbox.
