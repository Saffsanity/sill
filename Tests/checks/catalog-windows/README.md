# `catalog-windows`

**Checks:** which on-screen windows the catalog lists, the tiles in a device's window strip (#114): an app's window by its owner (none, or the window server's nameless one), its layer (the desktop's icons, the wallpaper, widgets, the Dock's, the menu bar's), its size (100 and 101 points each way) and its process (Sill's own); the helpers' windows left out: LayerProbeParent in any case, macOS's own interface whatever the title (each of the six owners, in any case, never an identifier that only starts or ends with one; the open and save panel service and other Apple helpers stay listed), a tool's untitled window (no title, an empty one, spaces, a newline and a tab; a tool's titled window and an app's untitled ones stay); this Mac's list as a read-only probe saw it on macOS 27.0.1 (the same seven tiles as before and no line; the probe's own window left out, with its line); Stage Manager's strip (four tiles before, none now, one line); the lines, once per owner and none for what was never a tile; native tab switches (#366): a window gone and a new one of its app at its frame, ±2 pt, is a switch (another app's, one 3 pt away, one that was there before, as of two windows of one app at one frame the one closed, are not; one new tab replaces one window; two groups switching in one look each their own), the new tab in its old tab's slot, and the switches still to follow (`mergedSwaps`, #368's review: a chain to the tab that shows now, from every tab before it; a switch back; none from a tab to itself; one to a tab no longer on the screen dropped; one from a window on the screen again dropped, but the streamed window's, owed to it while a switch in flight holds its follow back, which stands against a later one of it); 20,000 seeded random windows against the rule written out; then a stand-in Mac with tab groups of two apps, with a copy of the catalog's and the coordinator's lines around the real functions: 4,000 random sessions of tab switches, new tabs, tabs closed, tabs moved into windows of their own (looked at on both sides), windows opened and closed, a device's picks (of a tile whose tab was hidden since the look, too), the Desktop, switches in flight for one to three looks, the last device leaving and one connecting again, the stream held to the window the person expects (the tab group they picked, on the tab it shows) and the bar to its order
