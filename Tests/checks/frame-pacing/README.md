# `frame-pacing`

**Checks:** which frame each device is sent, at home and away, when a keyframe is asked for on its behalf and when it is dropped (`FramePacing`, docs/steps/2026-11-24-home-backlog.md): each rule at its edges (home's late frame at 1 s, its two-message delta rule, a one-off loss asked for at once and a second within 2 s spaced, the keyframe a device waits for withheld while late and asked for again once 16 KB or less waits; away's budget, floor and slack, hold cap and first keyframe), the spacing (2 s, 4 s beside a device at home that is not losing frames and since the last keyframe), the sweep's request, the ceiling (64 MiB at any time; the oldest frame 10 s at home, 30 s away, after the grace), the drain backstop (4 s after 8 at home, 15 after 15 away), the lines and `FrameTimes`; the rules as they were at main 644f03b8 written out and compared step by step on random inputs (away always the same; at home the same wherever no frame is late and no loss comes within 2 s of the last); and a model of a connection in virtual time (Sill's own queue, the kernel's send buffer, the link, one shared encoder, pongs and the sweep) that shows issue #359's backlog on the old home rule and none on the new one, for one connection on 6 Mbit/s and five on 4–9, every decision unchanged on fast links (a 400 and a 600 Mbit/s cable, 50 Mbit/s Wi-Fi) and away, the old storm behind a small send buffer gone, a slow device beside a fast one giving it no more keyframes than the encoder's own, a dip recovered from, still windows caught up and a dead link dropped
