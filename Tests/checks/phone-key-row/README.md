# `phone-key-row`

**Checks:** the key row of a phone held upright and its switch, Arrow Keys (issue #287, PR #315; `PhoneKeyRow`): `Sill.arrowKeys`, off by default, offered on an iPhone (the phone's arrangement, the iPhone Duo's cover too) and never on an iPad; off, the six caps that name a key, PhonePortraitLayout's six, the row as it was; on, the four arrows after shift in a row that scrolls, laid across PhonePortraitLayout's own row 3 at every width a phone's arrangement has (300 to 599.5 pt in half points, at two heights): every cap at least 44 pt wide, as many whole as fit so and six at most (maximal), 0.6 of the next showing past them, the row filled exactly, never wider than the six's caps; each named iPhone's numbers (six whole from 375 pt, 49.4 pt on a 402 pt iPhone, five of 45 pt at Display Zoom's 320) and the 44 pt bound itself; its ends (at rest the six whole where six fit, at the end the right arrow at the row's edge with shift and the arrows whole, cmd too where six fit); the fades at an edge exactly while caps lie past it, half a point the threshold, over the cut cap's part and the gap, at most half the row; VoiceOver's action on every cap (#293's words, "Show the arrow keys" and "Show the modifier keys") and the cap it moves VoiceOver to; the words of Settings › This iPhone › Keys; and 20,000 random rows against the same, with the degenerate ones

**Compiles:** `iOSClient/PhoneKeyRow.swift` and `iOSClient/PhonePortraitLayout.swift` (`swiftc`)
