**An iPhone's arrow keys, behind a switch: PR #315 finished (2026-11-18, branch `ready-315` from oyyblin's `phone-arrow-keys` at ff1f9da, main at eb48ac9 merged in).**
Issue #287 asks for arrow keys on an iPhone held upright. Two community pull requests answer it: #315
(oyyblin: the key row scrolls sideways, `esc tab ctrl opt cmd shift ← ↓ ↑ →`, the left arrow peeking in
at the edge) and #293 (addhumal: a second page of the row, a swipe away, with nothing on screen to say
so). The review of 2026-11-18 (~/Downloads/sill-handoff/next/feature-requests/pr-evaluations/keys.md,
"#315 and #293") chose #315 to carry them, if Noah agrees, and asked for 44 pt caps, a switch, #293's
VoiceOver action and a device check. Noah, 2026-11-18: "Anything that currently exists as a community PR
should be reevaluated now to see if it solves the request and of course to add toggles." His phone
layout of 2026-09-27 had taken the arrows off the iPhone's row on purpose (docs/iphone-portrait-plan.md:
"I would get rid of the arrow keys and spotlight button"), so the row he approved stays the default.
- Kept: oyyblin's commit 989686e and the merge ff1f9da as they are; `PhoneKeyStrip` (the row that scrolls,
  its view-aligned stops, the fades at an edge with caps past it, `-SillKeyScroll end`).
- What was added, and why:
  1. **Arrow Keys**, this iPhone's switch, off unless turned on (`PhoneKeyRow.arrowKeysKey`,
     `Sill.arrowKeys`): Settings panel › This iPhone › a group headed Keys, after Three-Finger Gestures and
     before Take the Tour, with the review's words under it: "Adds ← ↓ ↑ → after shift in the key row.
     Swipe the row to reach them." Off, the row is main's six equal caps (56 pt on a 402 pt iPhone), not
     the strip. Only where a phone's arrangement is drawn (`DuoLayout.phoneArrangement`: an iPhone, the
     Duo's cover); an iPad has no such row and no Keys group. The whole-screen Settings of draft #393
     (`settings-pane`) keeps This iPhone's rows, so the group moves with them into its device part.
  2. **Never a cap under 44 pt** (the review's first point). #315's caps were 49 pt at 402 pt and 37 pt
     at 320 pt (Display Zoom). `PhoneKeyRow.Strip`: as many whole caps as fit at 44 pt or wider, six at
     most, and 0.6 of the next past them: six whole of 49.4 pt from 367 pt wide (every iPhone but
     Display Zoom), five of 45 pt at 320 pt, where shift is then the cut cap at rest (the six alone are
     42 pt there on main).
  3. **#293's VoiceOver action** on every cap of the row that scrolls: "Show the arrow keys" scrolls to
     the end and puts VoiceOver on the left arrow; at the end, "Show the modifier keys" goes back to esc
     (`PhoneKeyRow.Copy`, `voiceOverFocus`). Moving VoiceOver along matters here: left on esc, its next
     swipe would scroll the row back.
  4. **A cap held while the switch changes** comes up (KeyRow's `.onChange(of: arrowKeys)`): the row it
     was in is gone, and no release would come from it.
  5. **A pure rule and its check** (the project's standard): `PhoneKeyRow` (iOSClient/PhoneKeyRow.swift,
     pbxproj A315/F315) and `Tests/checks/phone-key-row` with PhonePortraitLayout's own row, and its
     mutants.
- No wire change, nothing on the Mac.
