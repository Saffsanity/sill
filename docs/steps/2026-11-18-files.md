**Second run (2026-11-18): the device's sending (the plan's §14 step 5).** With it, files and photos go from an
iPhone or iPad to the Mac end to end; the Mac to a device, the drop on the picture and the status menu's card are
later runs.

- **DeviceFiles** (iOSClient, on `sill.files`): the session's one outgoing transfer. The sources' sizes are read off
  its queue under NSFileCoordinator (an iCloud file downloads; a cancel never waits for it), then the offer; once the
  Mac accepts, parts of 64 KiB read in place, never whole, hashed as they go, each file's end with its SHA-256, within
  FileFlow's window (the person's last input, away, the link behind). A folder, a file that cannot be read, 501
  files or more than 100 GB stop before any offer; a file that shrinks while it goes stops `cantRead` and the Mac is
  told. A transfer belongs to the connection it was offered on: that connection no longer the session's ends it
  `lost`. Sill's own copies (a photo's file, an Inbox copy) go once it ends, and at launch.
- **The uplink ledger** (SessionLink, the overview's §2.3): everything handed to the session's connection is counted
  until the connection has taken it, by lane; `send` hands at once as before; a file's messages go only through
  `sendFile`, while no fence or hold stands and the connection holds nothing it was handed, never into `waiting`
  (`UplinkLedger`, pure). So at most one part's message is ever ahead of the person's next input in Sill's own queue,
  and beyond that the window bounds it.
- **The session** (StreamClient+Files): the list's `files` and kind 16's `sessionPaired` decide the rows
  (FilePolicy.deviceOffers); a background task while a transfer runs (iOS's end of it stops it `background`); the
  device's auto-lock held while it progresses (DeviceIdlePolicy.Uses gains `transfer`); the session's own moves wait
  while one runs (`OutgoingFiles.holdsMoves`: from Direct Wireless, up to the cable, home from away; a path gone never
  waits); the session's end ends it `lost`. The hello still says nothing of files: this build takes none from the Mac.
- **The rows** (HostSettingsPanel's This ‹device›, before the clipboard's, as the overview's §2.2 orders the group):
  Send Files to ‹Mac›… (the system's Files picker), Send Photos to ‹Mac›… (PHPicker: originals, no library access),
  Stop Sending while one runs, the transfer's line, the footnote (where they go, or why disabled: an older Sill, the
  Mac's switch off, not paired). They move into settings-pane's whole-screen pane (#393) with the rest of This
  ‹device›: its device part.
- **The chip** (FileChip): in the clipboard chip's corner, which waits while it shows: "Sending ‹name› to ‹Mac›" with

**Proposed words** (for the release that ships it; not in any published page):
- What's New: "Send files and photos from your iPhone or iPad to your Mac: in Settings, Send Files or Send Photos,
  or share a file to Sill from another app. They land in Downloads › Sill on the Mac, marked as downloaded, and only
  between paired devices."
- Support page, "Files and photos": "In Sill's Settings on your iPhone or iPad, under This iPad, Send Files to
  ‹Mac›… opens the Files picker and Send Photos to ‹Mac›… the Photos picker. You can also share a file to Sill from
  another app's share sheet. Files go to Downloads › Sill on the Mac, or to the folder you chose in Sill's Settings
  there (General › Files). Photos and videos go as originals. Files go only between paired devices, never while the
  Mac is locked, and never through a server; every file is marked as downloaded on the Mac, so macOS checks it before
  it opens. To stop a Mac from taking files, turn off Share files with devices in Sill's Settings on the Mac."
- Privacy page: "Files and photos you send go directly from your device to your Mac, encrypted, and never through a
  server. Sill reads only the files and photos you pick or share, with no access to your photo library, and never logs
  their names."
- App Review notes (at most 300 bytes): "Files: Sill appears in the share sheet for any file (document type
  public.item, rank Alternate) and sends it, after the person confirms, to their own paired Mac. Photos come only
  through the system picker; no library access is asked."
