- **What #261 does (mrandika).** A two-finger pinch sent to the Mac as its own trackpad's magnify gesture
  (`InputEvent.magnify`, kind 8), so Maps, Safari or Preview zoom their own content; the Mac puts its pointer under
  the fingers first, as apps zoom about it, and posts the window server's gesture event (type 29, subtype 8, the
  magnification in field 113, the phase in 132), with a watchdog and the host's end closing a pinch left open. The
  device sends it only to a Mac whose window list says so, and `PinchRouting` decides who a pinch belongs to. The
  author tried it on an iPad Pro (iOS 27.0.1) against Sill.app on macOS 27.2 in Maps, Safari and Figma.
- **The merge.** Git's four conflicts (ci.yml and Tests/checks/README.md: main's, with

  stored settings; the project file's new IDs, AC12/FC12, free on main and in every open pull request). Beyond git
  (344865e0): the Debug build's `#if DEBUG` switch in StreamClient (from Full Screen, 1c2837a6) and the key-strokes

- **The wire, renamed before anything ships, and the turn (aa81e434).** The review: rotate goes in now, under the
  same flag and generation, so a device never needs two. `InputEvent.rotate(PinchPhase, x:, y:, degrees:)` (the
  turn since the last event, counterclockwise positive as NSEvent.rotation; a device's y runs down, so its angle
  turns the other way). `MagnifyPhase` is `PinchPhase` (its strings unchanged) and `WindowList.magnify` is
  `WindowList.pinch` (`Pinch.generation`, 1: both gestures); its doc says it is not kind 28's four-finger `pinch`.
  The rotate is the same gesture event as the magnify, subtype 5 with its degrees in field 114, by the same
  reverse-engineered table: it waits for a device test.
- **The Mac's side, pure (PinchGestures).** What InputInjector posts for each event: the pointer under a gesture as
  it begins (more than a point away, and only for the first of the two), every event of a gesture where it began
  (the PR posted each where the device said), a began over an open one ending it first, then the pointer's move,
  then the new began (the live run below showed the end posted after the move; fixed), a lone change or end posting
  nothing, the values bounded (a doubling; a quarter turn) and a value or place that is not a number dropped whole,
  the magnify and the rotate open together at one place, the watchdog's second and the host's end. The input log
  names both, with the place.
- **Finding 1, fixed: the pinch recognizer took the wrong touches.** It allowed `.pencil`, `.indirect` and
  `.indirectPointer` with UIKit's cancel and delays, under the default too: it tracked every Pencil touch and
  trackpad click on the picture, against the input view's rule that every finger recognizer is direct-touch only.
  Fingers now have no recognizer of their own for a pinch (the stroke rule decides them, below), and a connected
  trackpad's pinch and turn have one each that take its transform events only: they refuse every touch
  (`gestureRecognizer(_:shouldReceive:)`), never cancel or delay one, and begin only under Zoom on ‹Mac›.
- **Finding 2, fixed: a picture stuck zoomed.** Zoomed under Zoom Stream, then Zoom App chosen: `zoomTouch` returned
  at once, so nothing could unzoom it until another source. StreamScreen watches who fingers' pinch belongs to
  (`fingersPinch`) and gives the whole picture back as it changes hands, the choice or a Mac that takes the pinch.
- **Finding 3, fixed: scroll and magnify at once.** The pinch recognized beside the finger pan. Now one rule decides,
  StreamZoom.Stroke's: a stroke takes a target as its first finger lands (`Target.mac` under Zoom on ‹Mac›, from
  PinchRouting), and what the rule calls a pinch is the Mac's (`Owner.macPinch`) instead of the picture's. Its steps
  (`macSteps`): the Mac's magnify and, with Rotate on ‹Mac›, rotate, each opening at its own 12 pt from where the pair
  landed (the spacing's change; each finger's arc) and catching up, then changing by what moved since, ending with
  its pair, three fingers or the last lift, at the point where the fingers landed. With the turn on, fingers that
  turn more than their midpoint moves are a pinch as spread ones are; with it off nothing changes. A stroke is the
  Mac's scroll or its pinch, never both.
- **A pinch on a trackpad, at the pointer.** Under Zoom on ‹Mac› the on-screen trackpad's two fingers go through the
  same rule (`TrackpadSurface.pinchStroke`), out at the cursor; a pinch scrolls nothing, and its lift is no right
  click. A connected trackpad's go where iPadOS's pointer is. The pad learns whether the Mac takes the pinch through
  the environment (`macTakesPinch`), so Trackpad mode's pad over the picture (#222, draft PR #398) has it too, with
  no change to its call sites, and #398's pad passes a trackpad's `.transform` to the picture's input view, where the
  new recognizers take it.
- **The toggles (the review's rows 3 and 4).** Pinch, Zoom Picture | Zoom on ‹Mac›, Zoom Picture by default (the
  PR's own key, `Sill.pinchZoomsMac`), and under it while Zoom on ‹Mac› is, Rotate on ‹Mac›, a switch, off
  (`Sill.rotateOnMac`). Both this device's own, never sent; in This ‹device› after Three-Finger Gestures, where the
  PR put it, until the whole-screen Settings pane (draft PR #393) gives them its Touch and Zoom group. Shown for every
  Mac: one that cannot take the pinch shows Zoom Picture, dimmed, whatever is stored, with "Update Sill on ‹Mac› to
  send pinches to its apps." The words are the review's. A change from the PR: under Zoom Picture a connected
  trackpad's pinch goes nowhere, as before #261 (the PR sent it to the Mac whatever the choice, new behaviour with
  no switch); every pinch now follows the one choice (the review's decision 1).
- **The words, findings 3 and 4 of 11-09.** The PR's footnote said "The iPhone’s trackpad pinch always zooms the app"
  on an iPhone; the review's words replace it. "Stream" is "Picture", the app's word. The Mac's segment carries its
  name up to 12 characters and "the Mac" past that, as a segmented control neither wraps nor shrinks (a default to
  confirm). The tour's picture card says what a pinch does under Zoom on ‹Mac› ("Pinch to zoom what’s under your
  fingers on ‹Mac›, as its trackpad would."), and with Rotate on ‹Mac› "Pinch or turn two fingers…".
- **The floor.** StreamClient.sendInput drops a pinch or a turn for a Mac whose list does not say `pinch`, the last
  word after the surfaces' own asks. docs/COMPATIBILITY.md says it; the names join its list once a public build
  carries them.
- **Not changed, for Noah to confirm.** The defaults above; the pointer moved under the fingers for Zoom on ‹Mac›
  (decision 2, the PR's); under Zoom Picture a trackpad's pinch does not zoom the picture (decision 1's other half
  would change today's default, and new behaviour stays off unless turned on); the gesture event's private fields.
